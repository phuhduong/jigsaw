## Prompt 19 — not accepted unchanged

Run `6a8325fc8b074137add0be47fa2767c3` used the original sensor request, Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite, and no manual part/page hints. It finished **incomplete / sourcing partial** after 19 model calls and two corrections; the final review exceeded the remaining input allowance. This independent review used the actual catalog records and original manufacturer pages, without changing the run.

**Disposition: conditional compatibility, not an independently accepted saved design.** The core parts plausibly implement the device; this is not evidence of a tested hardware failure. The remaining problems are more than the rejected quotation alone:

- **Support accounting is inconsistent.** Saved D1S1 claims external module capacitance of 22µF plus 0.1µF using C1/C2. Both purchased parts are actually 10µF, and C1 is also assigned to the regulator's 5V input. One placement cannot supply both input and output bulk capacitance. Espressif's peripheral circuit shows the 22µF/0.1µF pair on 3V3, while its module schematic includes internal 10µF and other bypass capacitors. Therefore this audit does not claim that 10µF external output capacitance inevitably causes instability or that every typical circuit value is mandatory; the saved fulfillment claim nevertheless lacks a coherent source-backed justification. See the [WROOM datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-wroom-32e_esp32-wroom-32ue_datasheet_en.pdf), physical pp. 37 and 39.
- **USB source capacity is misrepresented.** The 5A figure is the [USB4105 connector](https://gct.co/files/specs/usb4105-spec.pdf) contact rating, p. 2, not an available USB supply contract. The saved 500mA MCU peak plus sensor/regulator demand also contradicts its under-500mA total assumption. WROOM p. 29 documents a 379mA RF peak under stated test conditions, so a workable 500mA sensor configuration is plausible; replacing the saved workload with that case would be a repair, not an unchanged-record pass. Two 5.1kΩ CC pull-downs are appropriate for a properly scoped power-only configuration; this finding does not mandate a USB controller.

Other checks are favorable: the exact WROOM/SHT40/AZ1117CH variants have compatible nominal supplies; 10kΩ I²C pull-ups and both logic directions are suitable for the stated standard/fast-mode options with appropriate compact-board capacitance. The sensor 100nF bypass and EN 10kΩ/1µF support values fit their functions. The [SHT4x datasheet](https://sensirion.com/media/documents/33FD6951/6A7C10A0/HT_DS_Datasheet_SHT4x_V7.3.pdf), pp. 3 and 9, supports these observations; heater-off should be explicit for its 1mA sensor budget.

Thermal screening is reasonable for the **saved** 50°C ambient, 200mA average, and source-conditioned 100°C/W estimate: approximately 0.403W pass-path loss, or 0.435W including 6mA regulator overhead, remains below the 0.65W allowance. The reviewer's approximately 70°C junction claim is wrong; approximately 93.5°C still passes the same 115°C target. Initial-output-voltage decimals likewise overstate the loaded guarantee, but do not establish a material supply mismatch. See [AZ1117C](https://www.diodes.com/assets/Datasheets/AZ1117C.pdf), pp. 4 and 7; the thermal estimate remains conditional on the documented copper/board arrangement, not PCB qualification.

**Traceability-only issue:** rejected D3E3 correctly describes AZ1117C's minimum 10µF output capacitance and ESR below 20Ω (p. 3). Its `10uF` quote differs from the source's `10µF`; at this trial's revision the quote helper rejected the former. The cleanup later normalized this specific numeric unit spelling, while still rejecting changed values/units. Direct configuration cannot repair a protected source packet. This historical rejection was retained; the run was not edited or relabeled.

Sourcing contains 13 placements / 10 MPN groups, with **$8.77 known parts subtotal** excluding C3 `CG0805X7R104KWT`, which has no offer/price/stock. Other groups have available cut-tape offers in the saved snapshot.

This single uncontrolled run does not establish that cleanup caused an engineering regression. Its support miscounting, source-versus-load confusion, and ineffective corrections repeat observed model failure modes; the applicable instructions prohibit them. All 32 pre-cleanup snapshots had unchanged deterministic findings and BOM projections in the separate read-only regression check.
