## Material claims versus minor issues

1. **Thermal screening is now coherent, with a source-conditioned cooling assumption.** The saved 50 C ambient, 110 C junction target and 100 C/W theta_JA produce a 0.6 W allowance. The declared 250 mA sustained load gives `(5.25-3.235) x 0.25 = 0.50375 W`, before regulator own-current loss. The cited 100 C/W value applies to the manufacturer's copper-cooled SOT-223 condition, not an uncooled board: 100 mm2 copper, two-layer 2 oz FR-4 and eight 0.5 mm vias. That is a legitimate pre-layout implementation condition. The datasheet's 6 mA no-load quiescent-current figure suggests about 0.032 W additional input loss as a rough screen, still within the allowance, but is not a guaranteed loaded-loss measurement. No forced steady peak-current thermal load or finished PCB qualification is needed here.
2. **The narrow regulator output envelope is still not established.** The saved 3.235-3.365 V bounds directly reuse a 10 mA test-load table conditioned on at least 1.5 V input-output differential. Minimum USB input minus the saved output maximum is only 1.385 V. The source also lists separate line/load regulation. Its 1.3 V maximum dropout at 0.8 A supports plausible operation, but does not establish that exact narrow envelope at every proposed load. A source-backed wider estimate or reconciled operating constraint is still needed; the source does not show that the actual regulator is intrinsically incompatible with the 3.0-3.6 V module and 1.08-3.6 V sensor.
3. **The current record confuses typical and maximum heater current.** SHT4x physical p9 says 6 mA typical and 10 mA maximum for the lowest 20 mW heater setting. The saved assumption instead calls 6 mA a heater peak, without choosing the heater setting. It states a 391 mA upstream sum, while the numeric input load remains 385 mA. These are real record errors. They do not demonstrate a failed 500 mA power source: even the documented 100 mA high-heater maximum plus the cited 379 mA WiFi-mode peak and 6 mA regulator allowance totals about 485 mA before other small loads. The actual supported operating mode and margin must nevertheless be stated consistently rather than silently repaired by this audit.
4. **One unnecessary resistor comes from a source misread.** Saved D1E11/D1S3 describe a manufacturer-recommended 10 kohm UART0 pull-up, fulfilled by R2. WROOM physical p39 actually shows R6 on GPIO16, explicitly conditional on a PSRAM module variant; it is not the UART pull-up claimed, and the selected N4 module does not need that PSRAM support. This is a minor extra $0.10 component and false rationale, not an omitted enable network or demonstrated functional incompatibility.
5. **Interfaces and USB support are otherwise plausible.** Both I2C driving directions have ample static logic margins, and a single sensor at 0x44 has no address conflict. The 3.9 kohm +/-1% pull-ups clear the sensor's low-state requirement and permit a practical compact bus; at 400 kHz the cited rise-time relation implies a bus-capacitance ceiling of approximately 90 pF. Off-board programming is a legitimate assumption without requiring numbered pins or a full header plan. Two actual 5.1 kohm CC resistors are present and match the retained TI USB power-only guidance, despite missing support links. The connector's labeled supplier current rating is ample for the assumed 500 mA source; its unavailable manufacturer download does not make the part inherently wrong.

The parts are close to a feasible BOM, but the saved result is neither fully sourced nor checked, and its voltage/current claims still need correction. No hardware was built or tested, and no physical failure is claimed. This audit does not promote it to checked or add an unstated heater, voltage-envelope or cooling rescue configuration.
